## 1. Initial Thoughts

The main users of QueueSmart are regular users and administrators. Regular users may include students, patients, customers, or anyone waiting to receive a service. Administrators are staff members responsible for creating services, managing queues, and monitoring the overall system.

To create an account, users will provide their first and last name and either a phone number or email. Once logged in, users can join or leave a queue and book an appointment through the QueueSmart web application. While waiting, users will be able to view their current queue status, including their estimated wait time and the number of users ahead of them.

Administrators will have their own accounts and access to administrative functions within QueueSmart. They will be able to create and manage services and queues, configure priority settings, and view statistics and queue history. QueueSmart will provide administrators with information such as average service times, current wait times, and usage statistics so they can monitor how efficiently services and queues are operating.

The most important features of QueueSmart are account management, queue management, appointment booking, estimated wait times, priority-based ordering, notifications, service management, and queue history. These features give users better visibility into their wait while giving administrators the information and controls needed to manage queues efficiently.

One of the main challenges will be providing accurate wait-time estimates because actual service times may be shorter or longer than expected. Another challenge will be handling long queues and priority levels while keeping users' queue positions accurate as people join or leave. Notifications will also need to be timely and reliable so users do not miss important queue updates. The system must also handle multiple users interacting with queues at the same time without displaying inconsistent or outdated information.

## 2. Development Methodology

Our team will follow the Scrum framework based on Agile development methodology. Agile is basically the mindset behind how we want to work — staying flexible, building things in small pieces, and adjusting as we go instead of trying to plan everything perfectly at the start. Scrum is how we’re actually going to do that throughout the semester. We’ll break our work into short sprints (around a couple of weeks each), keep a backlog of tasks we need to get done, and check in with each other regularly to see how things are going.

We think this approach makes sense for a few reasons. First, QueueSmart is made up of several smaller pieces like login/authentication, service management, queue management, appointment booking, notifications, and history tracking. That kind of project fits well with Agile because we can work on pieces one at a time instead of trying to build the whole thing at once. Second, since this class has us building the project across multiple assignments (A1 through A4, then the final demo), it already feels like we’re working in sprints whether we plan it that way or not. Each assignment is basically a checkpoint where we show our progress and get feedback. Third, and this is something we already ran into while working on our Initial Thoughts section, some of our biggest challenges, like getting wait-time estimates right, aren’t things we can completely figure out on paper. They depend on actual usage and data, so our plan is to start with a basic version, see how it performs, and improve it over time instead of trying to get it perfect on the first try. Lastly, since we’re a small team, Scrum works well because it doesn’t require a lot of overhead, long documents, or complicated processes — just regular check-ins and a clear list of tasks.

As far as how this helps us across the semester, we’re planning to treat each assignment like the end of a sprint, where we stop, look at what we’ve completed, and use any feedback before moving on to the next part. Since Agile is about adapting, if we realize that something we decided in A1 doesn’t work well once we’re deeper into A2 or A3, we can go back and adjust it instead of being stuck with our original choice. It also makes tracking contributions on GitHub easier because the work from each sprint can be aligned with the assignments, making it easier to document each team member’s contributions.

## 3. High-Level Design / Architecture

### How the Major Components Interact

QueueSmart is designed as a single web application that interacts with two main external actors: the **User** and the **Administrator**. The system also depends on an external **Notifications Service** that handles email or SMS notifications.

A **User**, such as a student or customer, interacts with QueueSmart to create an account using their first and last name and a phone number or email. Through the web application, users can join or leave a queue and book an appointment. QueueSmart provides users with their current queue status, including their estimated wait time and the number of users ahead of them.

An **Administrator**, such as a staff member, interacts with QueueSmart to manage services and queues, view statistics and history, and configure priority settings. QueueSmart provides administrators with information about queue performance, including average service times, current wait times, and usage statistics. This allows staff members to monitor current queues and better understand how services are being used.

QueueSmart also communicates with an external **Notifications Service**. When a queue event requires a notification, QueueSmart sends the relevant queue information to the Notifications Service. The external service uses this information to generate and deliver an email or SMS notification based on the user's queue status. The Notifications Service can also deliver relevant notifications to administrators when needed.

This architecture keeps QueueSmart as a single system boundary while showing the people and external services that interact with it. The User and Administrator communicate directly with QueueSmart, while notification delivery is handled by an external service. The diagram intentionally avoids internal implementation details such as frameworks, programming languages, databases, or APIs because its purpose is to show the overall context of the QueueSmart system.
